Financial Plan — Privacy Policy

Effective August 17, 2026

Financial Plan is a personal financial planner built to work entirely in your browser. Because the numbers you put in are about as private as data gets, this page explains exactly what exists, where it goes, and who can see it.

What Financial Plan Stores

Only what you put in: budget figures, account balances, savings goals, retirement and compensation figures — including what you plan to spend in retirement and any income you expect then — vacation costs, PTO plans, donations, holdings and any property values or cost bases you record, any tax brackets you enter on the Tax tab, and any notes; plus your preferences (theme, which tab was open, and which boxes you folded up). A net-worth snapshot you press Record on stores the figures the card was showing at that moment — a computed number you chose to keep, which is why "put in" rather than "type in". There is no account, profile, or usage tracking required to use the app.

The tax brackets are worth naming separately, because they are the one thing here that is about tax: they are a table you type, stored and synced exactly like every other figure above, along with the taxable share you give each retirement income row and any income you type into the tab's calculator. The app ships no brackets of its own, looks none up, and sends nothing anywhere to work tax out — it is arithmetic done in your browser over numbers you entered, and pasting a schedule in parses it on the spot without it leaving the page. Nothing here is a tax return, an assessment, or a figure you have paid.

Where That Data Lives

In your browser (always). Everything is stored in your browser's local storage on your own device. This is the only copy that exists unless you sign in to sync. Clearing the site's browser data — or the app's own "Delete all data" button — deletes it. If you fill in the Household tab, "everything" includes the names, roles and birth months of the people you add, your children among them — so the same care you would take over the figures applies to a backup file or a screenshot.

A copy of the app itself, so it opens offline. The page, its stylesheet, its chart library and its icons are kept on your device — the same public files anyone can read on GitHub, and nothing else. Your plan is never put there, nor is anything fetched from sync or from the price lookup. Clearing the site's browser data removes it along with everything else.

Google sign-in / Firestore (optional, off until you choose it). If you tap "Sign in to sync", your data is stored in a Firebase/Firestore database operated by the developer so the same figures can appear on your other devices, and Firebase Authentication records your Google account's email address and display name. Access rules restrict every account to its own data — other signed-in users cannot read yours, and the developer does not look at it. This only happens if you sign in; the app never does it on its own. "Delete all data" while signed in empties the synced copy as well as this browser's: the document is left holding a blank plan — no figure, no name, no birth month — and your other devices are asked whether to clear themselves too. The empty document itself, and the Firebase Authentication record of your email address and display name, remain until you ask for them to be removed (see Your choices below). What is synced is the whole plan as one document, so if you have filled in the Household tab that includes your family members' names and birth months, including any children's.

Holding price lookups (optional, off until you add a key). If you enter a Twelve Data API key in Preferences, the Investments tab can fetch current prices for the tickers you have recorded (any holding whose price lookup you have left switched on). Only the ticker symbol and your own key are sent to api.twelvedata.com — never a balance, a share count, or anything else about you. The key is kept in this browser only: it is deliberately not part of your synced data and not part of an exported backup, so you enter it once per device. Fetched prices are cached for six hours and remain editable by hand.

That six-hour cache — the ticker, the price fetched for it and the time it was fetched — is part of your plan, so if you are signed in to sync it reaches your other devices along with everything else, and it is included in an exported backup. That is deliberate: it is what stops a second device deciding a price is out of date and fetching it all over again minutes after the first one did. It is never included in a read-only share link. Delete the key in Preferences to switch lookups off; "Delete all data" removes the key and the cached prices along with everything else.

This used to use Alpha Vantage. Nothing about what leaves your browser has changed — still the ticker and your key, and nothing else — but the address it goes to has, and alphavantage.co is no longer contacted at all. Any Alpha Vantage key you had saved is deleted from this browser the next time the app opens, since nothing can use it any more.

Share links (nothing is uploaded). The Share button builds a read-only link that carries the figures inside the link itself, compressed into the part of a web address that is never sent to any server. Creating one and opening one both involve no upload, no account, and no storage on the reader's device — and opening one never touches whatever that reader already had saved in their own browser. You pick which tabs go in and how many years go with them; people's names are replaced with "Adult 1" and "Child 1" unless you tick the box to include them, and your written notes and the years you have built ahead are left out unless you ask for them. Anyone who has the link can read everything inside it and there is no way to withdraw one, so treat a link like the figures it contains.

What Financial Plan Does Not Do

  • No analytics or usage-tracking scripts
  • No advertising or ad networks
  • No selling or sharing data with third parties
  • Sharing the app's plain address shares only the app — your numbers travel only in a share link you deliberately create
  • The "Recent changes" list fetches this app's own public commit history from GitHub's API; nothing about you is sent with that request

Your Choices

You can edit or delete anything in the app at any time, and export a full backup from the Back up dialog. Signing out stops all syncing and leaves the local copy in charge. "Delete all data" while signed in empties the synced copy of everything it held. To have the document itself, and the Firebase Authentication record of your email address and display name, deleted from the developer's Firebase project, email eagleadams86@gmail.com and they will be removed.
